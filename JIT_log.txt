• For the Navier-Stokes solver itself: probably yes, if the only differences are last-digit floating-point roundoff from cuFFT choosing a different algorithm/order.

  For the optimization rule you were using: no, because that rule required equality to the baseline output, and Patient JIT changed printed diagnostics.

  The key distinction:

  - Physics/numerics OK: cuFFT Patient JIT can use a different FFT kernel plan. That changes floating-point summation order, so last-bit differences in velocity/vorticity fields, CFLM,
    CN, and DT are expected. For Navier-Stokes, especially turbulent or long-running cases, exact bitwise reproducibility is not usually a meaningful physical requirement.
  - Regression-test not OK: if your baseline comparison is exact or near-exact on printed values, even harmless ULP-scale drift fails.
  - Potential risk: if the tiny difference crosses an adaptive timestep, viscosity, clipping, or diagnostic branch threshold, the trajectory can diverge over many steps. That still may
    be physically valid, but it is no longer the same deterministic run.

  What I would do before allowing it in a performance branch: compare with tolerances on field norms and diagnostics, not exact stdout. For example, check relative/absolute error in
  energy, enstrophy, spectrum, final T/DT/CN/VISC, and maybe max norm of omega/u/v. If those stay within a chosen tolerance over representative runs, Patient JIT is likely acceptable
  scientifically.

  So: OK for a tolerant scientific solver validation; not OK for your current baseline-equality optimization loop.
